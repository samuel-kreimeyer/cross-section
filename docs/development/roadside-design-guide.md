# Roadside Design Guide (2011) - distilled rules for cross-section builders

This is a brief extraction of business-rule-relevant guidance from the Roadside Design Guide (2011) chapters in `docs/reference/roadside-design-guide-2011/`. It focuses on geometry, clear zones, and roadside hardware placement.

## Core principles
- Prioritize a forgiving roadside: remove, redesign to be traversable, relocate, use breakaway hardware, then shield with barrier; delineate only when other options are impractical.
- Clear zones are guidance ranges, not absolutes; use site-specific judgment (speed, ADT, slope, crash history, urban vs rural, constraints).
- Clear zone includes shoulders, bike lanes, and auxiliary lanes except those that function as through lanes.

## Clear zone and recovery area
- Clear zone is measured from the edge of the through traveled way; for low-volume roads without a defined edge line, measure from the right-wheel path.
- Use Table 3-1 for the base clear-zone distance by speed, ADT, and slope; treat it as a range center, not a fixed value.
- On horizontal curves, apply the clear-zone adjustment factor (Table 3-2) to the outside of the curve only.
- Non-recoverable slopes mean the clear zone does not end on the slope; provide a clear runout area at the toe.
- If the site is constrained and full clear-zone width is impractical, aim for uniform clear zone along a segment; prioritize locations with severe consequences (water, cliffs, fixed objects).

## Slopes and roadside geometry
- Recoverable foreslope: 1V:4H or flatter (use Table 3-1 clear zone directly).
- Non-recoverable foreslope: 1V:3H to 1V:4H; expect vehicles to reach the toe; provide clear runout area there.
- Critical foreslope: steeper than 1V:3H; if within clear-zone distance and cannot be flattened, consider barrier.
- For 1V:3H fill slopes, keep fixed objects away from the toe; provide a toe recovery area (3 m [10 ft]).
- Transverse slopes: 1V:10H desirable; 1V:6H max for high-speed roads adjacent to traffic, then can transition steeper further away.
- Drainage channels: prefer traversable cross sections (see Figures 3-6 and 3-7). If outside preferred envelopes, reshape, pipe, or shield.
- Cut slopes can be steeper if smooth and clear of obstacles for ~3 m [10 ft] above the ditch flow line.

## Urban and restricted areas (offsets)
- Curbs do not provide redirection above ~40 km/h [25 mph]; fixed objects near curbs are still hazardous.
- Minimum operational offset behind curb is 0.5 m [1.5 ft] for frangible items (not a clear-zone substitute).
- Lateral offset goals on curbed facilities: 1.2 m [4 ft] at tangents; 1.8 m [6 ft] on outside of curves.
- Without curbs: 2.4 m [8 ft] tangents; 3.6 m [12 ft] outside curves.
- Merge tapers: target 3.6 m [12 ft] offset from curb face near the taper point; breakaway items at least 1.2-1.8 m [4-6 ft].
- Driveways: keep objects 3.0-4.6 m [10-15 ft] beyond driveway edge; preserve sight triangles.
- Intersections: avoid rigid objects on channelization islands/median noses; use breakaway only. Target 1.8 m [6 ft] offset on curb returns (minimum 0.9 m [3 ft]).
- Guardrail behind curbs: place immediately adjacent per system spec or set back at least 2.5 m [8 ft].

## Work zones
- Work-zone clear zone is smaller than permanent clear zone due to constraints; typical widths 3.7-5.5 m [12-18 ft], with example table: 9.1 m [30 ft] at >=100 km/h [60 mph], 6.1 m [20 ft] at 70-90 km/h [45-55 mph], 4.6 m [15 ft] at 60 km/h [40 mph], 3.0 m [10 ft] at <=55 km/h [35 mph].
- Collateral hazards (equipment/material storage) should be outside larger clear zones (e.g., 9.1 m [30 ft]).
- No curve adjustment typically applied for work-zone clear zones.
- Portable concrete barriers: use MASH/NCHRP 350 compliant systems; connect segments; provide drainage; avoid protruding anchors.
- Temporary barrier flare rates: 4:1 to 8:1; 5:1 or 6:1 often preferred in urban areas. Minimum offset from lane to barrier: 0.6 m [2 ft].

## Barriers (roadside and median)
- Install a barrier only if it is less severe than the hazard being shielded; use benefit-cost where possible.
- Obstacles generally requiring shielding: bridge piers/abutments/rail ends, non-breakaway supports; others require case-by-case assessment (culverts, trees, utility poles, water bodies).
- For embankments, barrier need depends on fill height and slope (use RSDG Figure 5-1 or local agency modifications).
- Median barrier guidance (high-speed, fully controlled access):
  - Median <=9.1 m [30 ft] and ADT >20,000: recommended.
  - Median <15.2 m [50 ft] and ADT <20,000: optional; design to allow future barrier.
  - Median 9.1-15.2 m [30-50 ft] and ADT >20,000: consider study.
  - Median >=15.2 m [50 ft]: typically not unless crash history warrants.

## Breakaway supports (signs, luminaires, signals)
- Supports inside the clear zone should be breakaway or shielded; remove unused supports.
- Maximum stub height after breakaway: 100 mm [4 in.].
- Breakaway supports should not be placed in ditches; avoid steep slopes where the vehicle may be airborne. Acceptable foreslopes are 1V:6H or flatter; 1V:4H to 1V:6H only if the support face is within 600 mm [24 in.] of the shoulder-to-foreslope break.
- Large sign supports: hinge at least 2.1 m [7 ft] high; keep post weights within limits and avoid supplemental signs below hinge; posts within 2.1 m [7 ft] are treated as a group for breakaway performance.
- Luminaire supports: breakaway height <=18.5 m [60 ft], mass <=450 kg [1,000 lb]; avoid placement within barrier deflection distance.

## Terminals and crash cushions
- End treatments within the clear zone must be crashworthy (MASH/NCHRP 350) and must not spear, vault, or roll vehicles.
- Length-of-need (LON) point for many W-beam terminals is ~3.81 m [12.5 ft] from the nose; impacts upstream may gate through.
- Advance grading for terminals: <=1V:10H; adjacent grading should be near-flat, with ~1.5 m [5 ft] flat behind the nose; blend to ~1V:4H behind where practical.
- Minimum recovery area behind terminals: about 23 m [75 ft] long by 6 m [20 ft] wide (more for non-energy-absorbing systems).
- Flared terminals usually need ~1.2 m [4 ft] offset; tangent terminals often use 0.3-0.6 m [1-2 ft] offset.

## Bridges and bridge railings
- Bridge railings on NHS should be crash-tested and meet at least TL-3 unless a rational selection justifies otherwise.
- For TL-5 bridge rail, a concrete safety-shaped rail must be at least 1,067 mm [42 in.] tall and properly reinforced.
- Rigid bridge rails require approach guardrail and a compatible transition; attachments near rails must consider the zone of intrusion.

## Mailboxes (if modeled)
- Typical safe single mailbox: light-weight box on 100 mm x 100 mm [4 in x 4 in] wood post or 38-50 mm [1.5-2 in] pipe; embedment <=610 mm [24 in]. Avoid massive or rigid supports.
- Multiple mailbox supports must be breakaway; avoid rigid horizontal members at windshield height. Spacing between supports should be >= 0.75 of post height above ground. Prefer locating grouped units outside the clear zone.
- Mailbox bottom height: 1,040-1,140 mm [41-45 in].
- Lateral placement (typical):
  - Rural highways: provide 2.4-3.6 m [8-12 ft] shoulder/turnout; offset mailbox face 150-200 mm [6-8 in] behind shoulder edge.
  - Curbed residential street: mailbox face 200-305 mm [8-12 in] behind curb.
- Mailboxes should be on the right side in the carrier's direction; avoid expressways and high-speed/high-volume roads where possible.
